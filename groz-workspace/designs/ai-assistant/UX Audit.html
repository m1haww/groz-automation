01 / FLOW

The flow at a glance

From cold launch to a usable chat. Five screens of marketing before the user gets to type anything; three different home-shaped destinations after.

The user is asked to dismiss five screens before they're allowed to type a single message.

One brand, three voices.

The problem: three greens that don't sit on the same scale. Mint is calm and premium, action is loud and saturated, forest is illustrative. 03 / ONBOARDING

Five gates before first value.

The onboarding sells the app to someone who has already downloaded it. None of it teaches them how to use it, and none of it personalizes the experience.
1 2 3
Screen 03 · Welcome

Page-one is a billboard, not a doorway.

It announces the app to a user who already opened it. No personalization, no preview of what's inside, no choice — just a single CONTINUE that will lead to four more screens like it.

  • 1
    "Built on GPT-5.2 & GPT-5 Mini" is a vendor flex Medium
    Users care that the answers are good, not whose model is underneath. Lead with what the user can do, not the underlying API.
  • 2
    "100K+ users" + 5 stars + laurels High
    Social-proof furniture belongs on the App Store page. Inside the app it reads as insecure. The user already chose you.
  • 3
    CONTINUE button has no destination preview Low
    After tap, four more marketing screens appear. The user has no way to know that, and no way to skip.
Screens 04–05 · Feature carousel

App Store screenshots, shipped inside the app.

These screens duplicate the marketing material that convinced the user to download in the first place. They consume goodwill instead of building it.

  • 1
    Headline tone shifts from product to ad copy Medium
    "Find Answers to Any Questions", "Smart Ai-Camera Snap & Solve". The yellow-word emphasis is App Store-language; the rest of the app speaks in mint-teal product-language.
  • 2
    Mocks show features but not interactions Medium
    A floating bubble that says "Recognize → Get Answers" tells me the feature exists. It doesn't teach me how to invoke it. A 5-second video of the math camera in use would do real work; this static collage does not.
  • 3
    No "Skip" affordance High
    Even returning users have no shortcut. A user who already paid and reinstalled still gets the full five-screen tour.
  • +
    Bubble/sticker composition is charming Strength
    The "Recognize" + "Get Answers" callouts have personality. Worth keeping the device somewhere — just not as a forced page-turn.

Screen 06 · Reviews

The review wall is the weakest screen.

It does the most to undermine trust because it tries the hardest to manufacture it. Five anonymous testimonials, all five-star, all dated May 2023 — inside a 2026 app.

  • 1
    2023 dates inside a 2026 build High
    The dates are three years stale. Even if real, they read as either neglected or fabricated. Either reading is bad.
  • 2
    Five 5-stars in a row reads as planted High
    No 4s, no critique, no nuance. A user who has seen any App Store review section will recognize the pattern instantly.
  • 3
    "Join Millions of Happy AI Fans" is dissonant with the visual brand Medium
    The yellow-word callout language belongs in a different product. The mint-teal home screen is calm and assured; this is shouting.
04 / HOME & HUBS

Three homes, one indecision.

The bottom-tab spine is Home · Library · Log — but Home itself contains at least two distinct page templates, and Library duplicates the same job. The user can't form a mental model of where things live.

Search + 4 quick actions + Pro + Elite Tools
Home B — "AI Chatbot"
Assistants carousel + Popular Prompts
Library — "Tasks for AI"
Search + Pro + filter chips + 6+ posters
1 2 3 4 5
Home A · "Let's spark an idea"

Six sections, one screen.

In order down the page: hero, primary input, four quick-action cards, Pro upsell, "Elite Tools" carousel header, plus a teaser of the next carousel below the fold. That's six competing primary actions before scroll.

  • 1
    Hero greeting OK
    "Let's spark an idea." is warm and works. The colored word trick is fine in isolation; problem is it repeats elsewhere.
  • 2
    Composer + quick actions compete for the same job High
    "Ask AI anything…" and the "Ask AI" card below it route the user to the same place. One of them is redundant, and the user doesn't know which.
  • 3
    Four equal-weight tiles, no chosen path Medium
    Ask AI, Web Search, Text to Speech, AI Tasks. They are not at the same level of abstraction — "AI Tasks" contains the others. The grid implies parity that isn't true.
  • 4
    Pro upsell injected mid-content Medium
    Same banner appears on Library. Two upsells per session, before the user has produced any output, sets a coercive tone.
  • 5
    "Elite Tools" naming + premium implication Low
    "Elite" carries a paid connotation but the tools below aren't visibly gated. Either commit to "Pro-only" labelling or rename.
05 / CHAT

The empty state is the product.

This is the screen users will see most often — the one they land on every time they tap a model. Right now it's mostly black space.
Chat · empty state (ChatGPT, Coding Assistant, others)

A blank page is an anxiety, not a feature.

Every model lands the user on the same template: centered logo, one descriptive sentence, then a vast expanse of black above the composer. No suggested prompts, no recent prompts, no example of what "good" looks like.

  • 1
    The hero illustration is too small to do work Low
    At 80 px circle it's neither a launch graphic nor a useful affordance. Either commit to a hero illustration (300+ px, motion, characterful) or remove it.
  • 2
    No starter prompts High
    For a coding assistant: "Explain this stack trace", "Refactor this function", "Write a SwiftUI list". Even three suggestions cuts cold-start cost dramatically. Right now the user has to invent the entire interaction.
  • 3
    No model switcher in the chat Medium
    To change from ChatGPT to Qwen the user has to back out twice. The header is a great place for a one-tap model picker.
  • 4
    Composer chrome is heavier than the message it sends Low
    A teal hairline border, an image button, and a faint send button. Three weights of green on the same control. Simplify to: image · text · send, all one weight.

If the user does nothing, the screen tells them nothing. Empty states should always offer a next move.

06 / NAVIGATION

What does "Library" mean,
and what does "Log" mean?

The floating pill nav is visually distinctive. As an information architecture, it's working against the user.
Home tab active · "Home" label visible
Library tab active · "Library" label visible
Log tab active · "Log" label visible

Naming

  • A
    "Library" reads as content; it actually means task templates
    A user expecting their saved chats finds illustrated posters of Story / Coding / Interview / Math instead. Better candidates: Tasks, Workflows, Templates.
  • B
    "Log" reads as system logs; it actually means chat history
    For a chat product, history is the spine of the app. It deserves a louder name — Chats, History, or the model's own avatar with a count badge.

Behavior

  • C
    Only the active tab shows its label
    Users have to remember what the inactive icons mean. With three obscure icons (dots-cluster, stack, clock) that's a high memory cost. Show all three labels or use much clearer pictograms.
  • D
    Pill drifts horizontally as you switch tabs
    The Home position, Library position, and Log position have visibly different x-anchoring across screens 08–11 — the nav doesn't sit in a stable place. Small thing; reads as buggy.
  • E
    No tab for the user's current conversation
    If I'm mid-chat with ChatGPT and tap Library, can I get back? The nav vanishes inside a chat, so the answer is "back-arrow twice". A persistent chat anchor would help.
07 / SUMMARY

Findings, ranked.

Severity weighted by activation impact: how likely a first-time user is to bounce, or to never reach the moment of real value (a useful answer in a chat).
# What's wrong Why it matters Cheapest move Severity
01 Five-screen onboarding gauntlet before first chat Each tap is a churn point. Drop-off compounds; a user excited enough to download should reach value in <10 seconds. Collapse to one screen with a "Try a sample chat" CTA. Make the first chat itself the onboarding. High
02 Chat empty states with no suggested prompts This is the most-viewed screen in the app. Blank-page anxiety is the #1 reason new chat users disengage. Add 3–4 model-specific starter prompts under the hero. Tap inserts into composer. High
03 Fake-seeming review wall with 2023 dates Actively erodes trust at the exact moment you're asking for it (right before the paywall). Delete the screen. If social proof is needed, use a single quiet line on the paywall. High
04 Home A vs Home B vs Library — three competing hubs Forces the user to learn the IA before they can do anything. They will guess wrong twice. Pick one: either a chat-first home (composer + recent chats) or a task-first home (templates + recent chats). Not both. High
05 Pro upsell repeated mid-content on multiple tabs Trust is spent before the user has experienced enough to want more. One contextual upsell when a Pro-only action is attempted. Remove the always-on banners. Medium
06 Three greens + marketing yellow in the same palette Reads as unsettled. The brand has a moment (mint glow) — the rest fights it. Pick one mint as the brand color and one accent for CTAs. Delete the yellow callout system entirely. Medium
07 "Library" and "Log" don't describe what's in them Two of three tabs are mis-labelled. Discoverability suffers; recall fails. Rename to Tasks and Chats (or merge: chats with a "Templates" entry pinned at top). Medium
08 Five different card vocabularies Each in isolation is fine; together they signal a product still finding itself. Reduce to two: a "do something" tile and a "pick a thing" row. Apply consistently. Medium
09 No model switcher inside a chat Switching models is a core power-user move; right now it costs three taps. Tappable model name in the chat header → bottom sheet picker. Medium
10 Hero illustration on chat empty state is too small Doesn't justify its space. Either it carries the screen or it gets out of the way. Replace with starter-prompt cards (see #2). Reserve illustration for the model picker, not the chat. Low
08 / DIRECTION

What I'd change first.

If we get one sprint, these three moves matter more than the other twenty combined.
i.

Cut onboarding to one screen.

Welcome + a single tappable demo prompt that opens a real chat with the model. No carousel, no reviews, no paywall before value. The first answer is the pitch.

ii.

Make the chat empty state the hero.

Model name, brief identity, four starter prompts as tappable cards, recent chats below. This is the most-visited screen — design it like it is.

iii.

Decide what Home is for.

Chat-first (composer + recent + models pinned) or task-first (templates + recent). Pick one. Move the other to a single tab. Stop running both as competing home pages.

The product has a real visual identity worth keeping — the mint glow, the knot mark, the floating nav pill, the workflow posters. The work isn't to redesign the brand. It's to get out of its way so a user can talk to the AI within ten seconds of opening the app.
